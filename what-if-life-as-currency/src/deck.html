Time is money. Literally.

What if your life was your currency?

GIORDANO · ADVANCED TEACHER MICHELLE

Imagine a world where you pay for everything with your life.

Not with money. With years.

Remember that time is money.

— Benjamin Franklin, 1748

Benjamin Franklin wrote this in 1748, almost 300 years ago.

Today I’ll show you a world where it’s not a metaphor.

Gold Life

1971: money loses its gold

On August 15, 1971, President Nixon ended the dollar’s convertibility into gold, at 35 dollars per ounce.

From then on, money was backed only by trust.

In my world, that same year, scientists discover how to transfer lifespan, and governments back money with human life.

Everyone starts with 50 years

Everyone is born with 50 years. You earn time by working, and you can spend, lend or lose it.

The US Department of Transportation already values a “statistical life” at 14.2 million dollars in 2025.

Over an 80-year life, that’s about 20 dollars per hour. That’s my exchange rate.

Banks lend life
Parents give years
Thieves steal time
Prison takes time

Everything changes

Debt makes your life shorter.

Grandparents feel pressure to donate their years.

Crime and punishment are measured in time.

Money already buys years

This is the real part. Rich people already live longer.

In my own city, two neighborhoods are 20 years apart.

Ana, 16

Her mother: 8 months left

Would you selltwo years?

Ana works after school to earn days for her mother.

At the market, she sees a man buy 5 years from a stranger who needs to pay rent.

She has to decide: sell 2 years of her own life?

Waste time

Borrowed time

But time never comes back.

Our language already thinks this way

Lakoff and Johnson, in “Metaphors We Live By” from 1980, showed that “time is money” is a metaphor that shapes how we think.

But there’s one difference: money can come back, time can’t.

In my world, even that difference disappears.
